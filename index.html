What we do

Threat research

Reports on ransomware crews and state-backed groups, including Qilin, Scattered Spider, and the Lazarus Group.

Competitions

We play capture-the-flag events year-round as ECX Inc., the team we run with WMU's Computer Club. We also ran our own CTF, WRAVEN CTF 2026, with a bracket for high school teams.

Watchtower

A public threat-intelligence dashboard that we run on OpenCTI. It pulls in public feeds such as CISA's list of exploited vulnerabilities and MITRE ATT&CK.

Tools we write

slantwise (beta) estimates where an audio recording was made from the propeller planes in the background. field-ops-docs holds the cheat sheets we sync to our Kali machines.

Questions

Is WRAVEN a hacking club?

We study attacks so that we can explain them and defend against them. Members test only systems that they own or have written permission to test. Every member signs our ethics agreement.

What happens when a member finds a vulnerability in someone else's system?

It goes to our Compliance Officer before anything else happens. The owner hears from us privately, and the default wait before we publish is 90 days. WRAVEN does not sell vulnerabilities, and does not trade them privately. A coordinated disclosure with a software vendor started in October 2026 and is still in progress.

I found a vulnerability in a WRAVEN system. What do I do?

Email contact@wraven.org, encrypted with our PGP key if it is sensitive, and read our disclosure policy. It protects good-faith reports.

Can WRAVEN review our security?

Yes. WRAVEN does on-premises security reviews and attack surface audits. Before any work, we agree with you in writing on what we will look at, and what we find goes to you privately. Tell us what you need.
