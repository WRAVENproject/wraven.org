At a meeting

Meetings are hands-on. Workshops cover traffic analysis, OSINT (open-source intelligence), reverse engineering, web exploitation, and vulnerability analysis.

We also practice for capture-the-flag (CTF) competitions, give demos, and hold study sessions for security certifications. Hardware workshops use the club's own gear for Wi-Fi hacking, sub-GHz radio, RFID, and other RF work. Each semester has events for beginners.

Your first meeting

At your first meeting, you sit down with an officer. You get your WRAVEN accounts, join the Discord, and sign the ethics agreement.

Questions

Do I have to be a computer science major?

No. Any WMU student can come, of any major and any year.

Is it free?

Yes. Everything is free. WRAVEN has recommended dues, but you do not have to pay them to take part.

What if I miss a week?

Come to the next meeting, and ask on the Discord what you missed.

Which systems am I allowed to test?

Systems that you own, systems that WRAVEN owns, and systems whose owner has given explicit written authorization. The WMU campus network, WMU systems, and other students' devices are never authorized targets. The ethics agreement has the full rules.

For anything else, ask on Discord or email contact@wraven.org.
